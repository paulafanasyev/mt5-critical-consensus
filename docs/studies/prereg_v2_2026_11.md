## 5. Rules under test (exactly three, no additions, no parameter search)

Common to all three. Decisions use closed bars only: a signal evaluated at the close of bar
`i` is filled at the **open of bar `i+1`**, never at the signal bar's close. One position per
instrument; no pyramiding; no reversal while a position is open; **no new signal is evaluated
on the bar on which a position is closed** — the next candidate signal is the bar after the
exit, filled at the open of the bar after that. The broker's own trading calendar defines
bars; there is no session filter and no weekend trading, because daily spot bars do not exist
on weekends. Holidays simply have no bar, and the gap rules handle the jump.

**Exit geometry, identical for every candidate.** At entry, `risk = 2 × ATR14(signal bar)`,
where ATR is the simple mean of true range over the last 14 bars including the signal bar.
Long: `stop = entry − risk`, `target = entry + 2 × risk`; short mirrored. Exits are checked
from the entry bar itself, and within any bar:

1. if the open is through the stop → fill at the **open**;
2. else if the open is through the target → fill at the **open**;
3. else if the bar trades through the stop → fill at the **stop**;
4. else if the bar trades through the target → fill at the **target**.

Both gap cases are resolved before any intrabar level, because the open is chronologically
first; only when the open is inside both levels does the stop-before-target rule apply, so a
bar that contains both is scored as the stop. A trade still open on the window's last bar is
closed at that bar's close and recorded with reason `end`; it counts in `n`.

**Time exits, counted exactly.** `max_hold = H` means the entry bar `E` is held bar 1,
protective levels are live on bars `E … E+H−1`, and if neither was touched the position is
closed at the **open of bar `E+H`**. The window end overrides a time exit that would fall
after it.

**C1 — inside-bar compression breakout (XAUUSD only, daily).**

- Warm-up: no signal before bar index 30 of the series (ATR(14) is then a full-window mean).
- Compression: bar `i−1` is *inside* bar `i−2` iff `high[i−1] ≤ high[i−2]` and
  `low[i−1] ≥ low[i−2]`.
- Signal at the close of bar `i`: long if `i−1` is inside **and** `close[i] > high[i−1]`;
  short if `i−1` is inside **and** `close[i] < low[i−1]`. Both cannot hold.
- Entry: open of bar `i+1`; stop and 2R target as above; `H = 10`.
- Rationale: volatility compression precedes expansion, and the trigger is the compressed
  bar's own range, not a 20- or 55-day extreme, so this is not the rejected Donchian rule.
- Known confound, stated in advance: gold rose over the window, so a positive result must be
  read beside the long/short split reported with it (a pass carried by the long side alone is
  a drift result, not a compression result).

**C2 — gold/silver ratio reversion (XAUUSD traded, XAGUSD as signal, daily).**

This is a ratio-reversion rule, not a lead-lag regression: no lag coefficient is estimated, so
a pass would say "the five-day gold/silver relative return reverts after a 1.5-sigma
dislocation", not "silver leads gold".

- Joint observations: dates on which both venues have a bar, ordered by date, `k` counted from
  zero. For `k ≥ 5`: `g(k) = (XAU[k]/XAU[k−5] − 1) − (XAG[k]/XAG[k−5] − 1)`, both returns
  spanning the **same pair of joint dates**.
- Standardise on the 60 most recent prior gaps: `m = mean(g(k−60 … k−1))`, `s = sample
  standard deviation (n−1)` of the same 60, `z = (g(k) − m)/s`. If `s ≤ 0`, no signal.
- Signal at `k ≥ 65`, i.e. the first possible signal is the 66th joint observation: long gold
  if `z ≤ −1.5` (gold has lagged silver), short if `z ≥ +1.5`.
- Entry: open of the next gold trading day; stop and 2R target; `H = 5`.
- Confound, stated in advance: the position is outright gold, so a positive result carried by
  the long side in a rising market is gold beta, not ratio reversion; the long/short split is
  reported with every result for this reason.
- The 60 gaps are overlapping five-day returns — roughly twelve independent blocks — so `s` is
  a noisy scale. That is a property of the pre-registered rule, not a defect to repair after
  seeing results.

**C3 — FX monthly time-series momentum (EURUSD, AUDUSD, NZDUSD, daily).**

- For the **last trading day of each calendar month**, with bar index at least 252 (0-based, so
  253 bars of history): `R252 = close / close 252 trading days earlier − 1` on the same
  symbol. `R252 > 0` → long, `< 0` → short, `= 0` → no position.
- Entry: open of the first trading day of the following month.
- Exit: 2R target, protective stop, or the **rebalance** — the open of the first trading day
  of the month after that. The rebalance is a time exit, not a signal.
- The window end overrides the rebalance: the final trade is marked at the last close with
  reason `end`, and that fact is reported.
- **The one exception to the re-entry rule.** A maturing position closes at the open of the
  first bar of month M+1, and the position for M+1 is entered at that same open, from a signal
  formed on the last close of month M. That is not a look-ahead — the signal predates the
  open — but it is the only place where an exit and an entry share one price, so it is
  written down here rather than left to the reader. Each side pays its own round trip.
- **Why these three pairs.** GBPUSD and USDJPY were requested on 2026-11-01 and the terminal
  returned no daily history for either (`copy_rates_range` → `Terminal: Call failed`, while
  their H1 history did serve), so the FX sample is the three majors whose daily history was
  available. The selection is by data availability, not by result, and it is a real
  limitation: all three are USD-quoted, so they share a dollar factor. The month clustering in
  §7 is what keeps the interval honest about that.

## 6. Costs

Charged once per round trip and subtracted from gross P&L before R is computed:

```
transaction = max(bar.spread, floor_points) × point + 2 × slippage
financing   = entry_price × rate_per_year × nights_held / 252
cost        = transaction + financing
```

| Symbol | point | spread floor | slippage per side | financing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| XAUUSD | 0.01 | 20 points ($0.20) | 5 bp of price | 3.0 %/yr |
| XAGUSD | 0.001 | 30 points ($0.03) | 5 bp of price | 3.0 %/yr (signal only, never traded) |
| EURUSD, AUDUSD, NZDUSD | 0.00001 | 2 points (0.2 pip) | 0.5 pip | 2.0 %/yr |

Why these numbers, and where they are weak:

- The D1 `spread` field is a single quote and is zero on a third of the bars, so a floor does
  most of the work. The floor is a 2026-era constant applied to a 2004–2019 sample in which
  gold rose about fourfold, so it is relatively heavier early in the sample. That direction
  flatters the early years, which is where the sample starts.
- 5 bp per side on metals is punitive for a fill at the daily open; 0.5 pip per side on the
  majors is several times typical slippage.
- Financing is charged in **both** directions at a flat rate. For metals that is optimistic:
  a long XAUUSD normally pays carry, and 3 %/yr is at the low end of retail swap. For FX it is
  direction-blind in both ways: a long AUD or NZD in 2004–2019 received the carry
  differential, and a short paid more than 2 %. The model undercharges shorts and overcharges
  those longs. Since a flat both-sides charge is the only implementable option without a carry
  series, the bias is stated rather than corrected.
- Not modelled: commission (assumed inside the spread), Wednesday triple swap, exit-bar
  spread widening, and the difference between the daily open and the first tradable print of
  the week.

A cost sweep (0×, transaction-only, base, double) is reported with every result. **The
decision uses the base cost and additionally requires the edge to survive at double cost**
(§8), so a result that lives inside the uncertainty of the cost model cannot pass.

## 7. Metric, interval and multiplicity

- **Primary metric: mean net R per trade**, `R = (gross P&L − transaction − financing) /
  (2 × ATR14)`. R is dimensionless and net of every charge above.
- **Interval: moving-block bootstrap over entry months, block length 3, 10 000 resamples,
  seed `20261101`.** Months are sorted chronologically and three consecutive months are drawn
  together with wrap-around. A month is the unit because positions that share a month share a
  cause: the three FX pairs enter on the same day, and a burst of gold trades is one
  dislocation. Blocks of three carry the serial dependence between adjacent months, which an
  independent-month bootstrap would drop. A month with no trade is not a cluster. What this
  still cannot capture is dependence across more than three months (a multi-year trend
  regime), so the interval remains narrower than a regime-aware interval would be; that is a
  stated limitation, not a repaired defect. Relative to an i.i.d. month bootstrap the block
  widens the interval, so the detectable-effect figures below are, if anything, optimistic.
- **Multiplicity:** three candidates. A **two-sided 98.33%** interval leaves 0.83% in each
  tail, so each candidate is effectively a one-sided 0.83% test and the family-wise error is
  at most 2.5% — stricter than the 5% the protocol allows. The confidence is kept as
  registered; the arithmetic is corrected here.
- **Minimum sample: 100 development trades and 30 clusters** (about ten blocks of three);
  the holdout floor is 30 trades and the same 30 clusters. Below either, the verdict is
  `no_decision`, which is a failure to deliver evidence, not a pass.
- **Power, stated in the units the test actually uses.** The lower bound clears zero only if
  the mean exceeds roughly `z·sd/√(clusters)` with `z = 2.394` and `sd ≈ 1.2 R`. So: 100
  monthly clusters detect ≈ 0.29 R; 180 clusters ≈ 0.21 R; 40 clusters ≈ 0.45 R. C3's 540-odd
  trades are only ~180 clusters, so its detectable edge is ≈ 0.21 R, not the 0.11 R a
  trade-count calculation suggests. The design is powered for large, cost-surviving edges
  only; a null result means "no edge of that size", not "no edge".

## 8. Decision rule and the one holdout look

1. Run development **once** for all three candidates and record it in
   `docs/evidence/candidates_dev_2026_11.json`.
2. A candidate **passes development** iff `n ≥ 100`, `clusters ≥ 24`, the lower bound of its
   98.33% cluster-bootstrap interval is above zero, **and** its mean net R is still positive
   at double cost.
3. **Only then** is the holdout opened, for that candidate alone. The tool refuses otherwise:
   `--window holdout` requires a development-evidence file whose recorded decision for that
   candidate is `passed: true` **and whose recorded n, cluster count, mean and lower bound the
   engine reproduces when it re-runs development**. It then writes a lock to the tracked file
   `docs/evidence/holdout_locks.json` carrying three sha256s — the evidence, this engine, and
   the data manifest — and refuses a second opening. Scoring a holdout requires a lock whose
   three hashes still match, so editing the rule, the data or the verdict after the look
   invalidates it. This is tamper-evident, not tamper-proof: a determined operator can still
   edit the code, delete the lock and re-run, but the deletion and the diff are in git.
4. A candidate **passes the study** iff the holdout also has `n ≥ 30`, `clusters ≥ 30`, and
   the lower bound of its 90% interval is above zero, with rules, costs and parameters
   unchanged.
5. A candidate that fails development is a completed deliverable with verdict **FAIL**; its
   holdout stays closed and is recorded as not opened.
6. No rule may be added, removed, re-parameterised or re-run after step 1. A code defect may be
   fixed only if it is reported with the diff and every candidate is re-run from scratch, and
   that fact is recorded.

**Holdout power, stated before the look.** With `n ≥ 30` and a 90% interval, the detectable
edge is ≈ 0.36 R; with the 34 monthly clusters the 2024–2026 window can hold, it is ≈ 0.34 R.
A holdout failure below that is **inconclusive**, not disconfirmation, and the verdict will say
so rather than record "did not survive out of sample" when the sample, not the edge, failed.
Every result therefore prints its own `detectable_r` next to the interval, and the verdict text
uses it.

## 9. What counts as "no"

- All three fail development → "no edge of the detectable size in these three families on the
  2004–2019 daily data", and the study stops. The holdouts stay closed.
- A candidate passes development but fails the holdout → "the development result did not
  survive out of sample", unless the holdout sample is too small to decide, in which case:
  "the holdout was too small to decide, and the development result remains unconfirmed".
- A candidate passes both → "positive mean net R with an interval above zero on this sample,
  in a holdout window observed at the market level and, for C3, in its trend family". It is
  not a promise of future profit, and no document may present it as one.

## 10. Out of scope

- Any search over parameters. The numbers in §5 are fixed; the code exposes no grid, and the
  only sweep re-prices the same trades against different cost multiples.
- Any rule added after seeing a development result.
- Treating the demo forward run, or any live demo result, as evidence of edge.
- Any claim of guaranteed or expected profit.
- Position sizing, drawdown tolerance or account risk: the study scores R, not an equity curve.

## 11. Reproducibility

```
python scripts/fetch_daily_basket.py --check          # verifies the five sha256s
python scripts/candidate_study.py --window dev --cost-sweep --json docs/evidence/candidates_dev_2026_11.json
python -m pytest tests/test_candidate_study.py -q     # 39 tests: fills, parity, no look-ahead, locks
```

`scripts/candidate_study.py` holds the engine and the constants (`WINDOWS`, `COSTS`,
`FINANCING_PER_YEAR`, `WARMUP_C1`, `WARMUP_C3`, `DEV_CONFIDENCE`, `MIN_DEV_TRADES`,
`MIN_CLUSTERS`). The three independent implementations in the test suite are written from this
document, carry their own ATR, their own constants and their own cost arithmetic, and compare
**entry, exit, direction, exit reason and R** — not just dates. Note that the suite executes the
development simulation; it prints no statistic, so it cannot leak a result, but it is why the
window is "unexamined" rather than "untouched".
