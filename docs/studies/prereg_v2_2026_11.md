# Pre-registration — three daily candidates, stamped 2026-11-01 (second attempt)

**Status: OPEN, awaiting a development run.** This document supersedes
`docs/studies/gold_2026_11_prereg.md`, which was rejected on 2026-11-01 because its holdout
had already been examined and its six clauses were ambiguous. Both defects are addressed
here: the development window predates every artefact in this repository, and every rule is
specified down to the fill so that two independent implementations can be compared trade by
trade (and are, in `tests/test_candidate_study.py`).

The stamp fixes the rules, the split, the costs and the decision rule. None of them may move
after a development result is seen. If the data says no, this study reports no.

## 5. Rules under test (exactly three, no additions, no parameter search)

Common to all three: decisions use closed bars only; a signal evaluated at the close of bar
`i` is filled at the **open of bar `i+1`**, never at the signal bar's close. One position per
instrument; no pyramiding; no reversal while a position is open. The broker's own trading
calendar defines bars — there is no separate session filter and no weekend trading, because
daily spot bars do not exist on weekends. Holidays simply have no bar; the gap rules below
handle the resulting jump.

**Exit geometry, identical for every candidate.** At entry, `risk = 2 × ATR14(signal bar)`
where ATR is the simple mean of true range over the last 14 bars including the signal bar.
Long: `stop = entry − risk`, `target = entry + 2 × risk`; short mirrored. Exits are checked
from the entry bar itself, and within any bar:

1. if the open is already through the stop → fill at the **open** (worse than the stop);
2. else if the bar trades through the stop → fill at the **stop**;
3. else if the open is already through the target → fill at the **open** (better);
4. else if the bar trades through the target → fill at the **target**.

The stop is checked before the target, so a bar containing both is scored as the stop. This
is the conservative ordering, and it is the one the code implements and the tests pin.

**C1 — inside-bar compression breakout (XAUUSD only, daily).**

- Warm-up: no signal before bar index 30 of the series.
- Compression: bar `i−1` is *inside* bar `i−2` iff `high[i−1] ≤ high[i−2]` and
  `low[i−1] ≥ low[i−2]`.
- Signal at the close of bar `i`: long if `i−1` is inside **and** `close[i] > high[i−1]`;
  short if `i−1` is inside **and** `close[i] < low[i−1]`. (Both cannot hold.)
- Entry: open of bar `i+1`; stop and 2R target as above.
- Exit: stop, target, or a **time exit after 10 bars** — if neither protective level was hit
  by the close of the 10th bar after entry, exit at the open of the next bar; if the window
  ends first, exit at the last close.
- Rationale: volatility compression precedes expansion; the trigger is the compressed bar's
  own range, not a 20- or 55-day extreme, so this is not the rejected Donchian rule.

**C2 — silver→gold lead-lag (XAUUSD traded, XAGUSD as signal, daily).**

- Joint observations: dates on which both venues have a bar, ordered by date.
- For joint step `k ≥ 5`: `g(k) = (XAU[k]/XAU[k−5] − 1) − (XAG[k]/XAG[k−5] − 1)`, both
  returns spanning the **same pair of joint dates**.
- Standardise on the 60 most recent prior gaps: `m = mean(g(k−60 … k−1))`, `s = sample
  standard deviation (n−1)` of the same 60, `z = (g(k) − m) / s`. If `s ≤ 0`, no signal.
- Signal at joint step `k ≥ 65`: long gold if `z ≤ −1.5` (gold has lagged silver), short if
  `z ≥ +1.5`.
- Entry: open of the next gold trading day; stop and 2R target as above; **time exit after
  5 bars**.
- Rationale: the metals complex co-moves and silver is the higher-beta leg; a standardised
  5-day dislocation is a catch-up trade, not a trend or a band rule.

**C3 — FX monthly time-series momentum (EURUSD, AUDUSD, NZDUSD, daily).**

- For the **last trading day of each calendar month**, `R252 = close / close 252 trading days
  earlier − 1` on the same symbol. `R252 > 0` → long, `< 0` → short, `= 0` → no position.
- Entry: open of the first trading day of the following month.
- Exit: 2R target, protective stop, or the **rebalance** — at the open of the first trading
  day of the month after that. The calendar exit is a time exit, not a signal.
- Rationale: time-series momentum in currencies is one of the most replicated anomalies in
  the literature; the point of the test is whether it survives this cost model, not whether
  it exists in a paper.

## 6. Costs

Charged once per round trip and subtracted from gross P&L before R is computed:

```
transaction = max(bar.spread, floor_points) × point + 2 × slippage
financing   = entry_price × rate_per_year × nights_held / 252
cost        = transaction + financing
```

| Symbol | point | spread floor | slippage per side | financing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| XAUUSD | 0.01 | 20 points ($0.20) | 5 bp of price | 3.0 %/yr |
| XAGUSD | 0.001 | 30 points ($0.03) | 5 bp of price | 3.0 %/yr (signal only) |
| EURUSD, AUDUSD, NZDUSD | 0.00001 | 2 points (0.2 pip) | 0.5 pip | 2.0 %/yr |

Why these numbers: the D1 `spread` field is a single quote, so a floor prevents a quiet bar
from pretending the spread was one point; 5 bp per side on metals is deliberately punitive
for an entry at the daily open; 0.5 pip per side on the majors is already several times
typical slippage. Financing is charged in **both** directions at a flat rate, which
understates carry received on a short AUD or NZD position and overstates it on a long — the
conservative asymmetry is intentional. Example: gold at $1 500 pays about $0.20 of spread
plus $1.50 of slippage, i.e. 0.11 %, and about 0.12 % more for a 10-night hold.

A cost sweep (0×, transaction-only, base, double) is reported with every result so the reader
can see the margin. **The decision uses the base cost only.**

## 7. Metric, interval and multiplicity

- **Primary metric: mean net R per trade**, where `R = (gross P&L − cost) / (2 × ATR14)`. R is
  dimensionless and net of every charge above.
- **Interval: 98.33 % two-sided cluster bootstrap**, 10 000 resamples, seed `20261101`. A
  cluster is one decision date (C1, C2) or one calendar month (C3), so trades that share a
  cause are resampled together. This protects against treating three correlated FX positions
  or five overlapping gold trades as independent draws.
- **Multiplicity: three candidates**, one-sided family-wise 5 % → each candidate must show a
  98.33 % interval lying entirely above zero. No other test is counted; no secondary metric
  can promote a candidate.
- **Minimum sample: 100 development trades.** Below that the verdict is "no decision", which
  is a failure to deliver evidence, not a pass.
- **Power, stated honestly:** the threshold is roughly `2.13 × sd / √n`. With the ~1.2 R
  dispersion these rules show, 150 trades can only detect a mean edge of about 0.21 R, and
  500 trades about 0.11 R. The design is deliberately powered for large, cost-surviving edges
  only; a null result means "no edge of that size", not "no edge".

## 8. Decision rule and the one holdout look

1. Run development once for all three candidates and record it in `docs/evidence/`.
2. A candidate **passes development** iff `n ≥ 100` and the lower bound of its 98.33 %
   cluster-bootstrap interval is above zero.
3. **Only then** is the holdout opened, for that candidate alone, through
   `scripts/candidate_study.py --window holdout`, which writes a lock to
   `agent_data/holdout_locks.json` before reading the window and refuses a second opening.
4. A candidate **passes the study** iff the holdout also has `n ≥ 30` and the lower bound of
   its 90 % interval is above zero, with the rules, costs and parameters unchanged.
5. A candidate that fails development is a completed deliverable with verdict **FAIL**; its
   holdout stays closed and is recorded as not opened.
6. No rule may be added, removed, re-parameterised or re-run after step 1. A defect in the
   code may be fixed only if it is reported with the diff and re-run from scratch for all
   three candidates, and that fact is recorded.

## 9. What counts as "no"

- All three fail development → the study reports "no edge of the detectable size in these
  three families on the 2004–2019 daily data", and stops. The holdouts stay closed.
- A candidate passes development but fails the holdout → "the development result did not
  survive out of sample", and that is the recorded result.
- A candidate passes both → the recorded result is "positive mean net R with an interval
  above zero on this sample, in a holdout window that had been observed at the market level
  but not for this rule family". It is not a promise of future profit, and no document may
  present it as one.

## 10. Out of scope

- Any search over parameters. The numbers in §5 are fixed; the code exposes no grid, and the
  only sweep in the code re-prices the same trades against different cost multiples.
- Any rule added after seeing a development result.
- Treating the demo forward run, or any live demo result, as evidence of edge.
- Any claim of guaranteed or expected profit.
- Any statement about position sizing, drawdown tolerance or account risk; the study scores R,
  not an equity curve.

implementations of C1, C2 and C3 are compared trade by trade in the test suite, which is the
concrete answer to "would two programmers produce the same trades from this document?".
